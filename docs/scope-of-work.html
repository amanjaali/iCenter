§1

What the system is for

One page. If you read nothing else, read this.

ALLVA runs eTrackify, a school bus tracking service. Bus companies pay ALLVA a monthly fee for every student their buses carry. Cyber Gate, ALLVA's technology partner on the project, takes half. What is left, after ALLVA has paid its own costs, is divided between five partners who each own a fifth of the company.

The system exists to make one promise keepable:

The objective

No item of income or expense can be lost, misrecorded, or left untracked — and all five partners can see the whole financial activity of the company.

The whole business in one line

StepWhat happensWho does it

InvoiceOnce a month the system counts the students and produces one invoice per bus company.Accountant

SpendSalaries, rent, SIM cards, fuel — everything ALLVA pays out is entered.Accountant

The four people who use it

RoleTheir job in one sentence
Operations Keeps the register of bus companies, buses and students up to date. Sees no money anywhere in the system.
Accountant Issues the invoices, records the payments and the costs, and presses the three month-end buttons.
Administrator Sets the price, creates the user accounts, and decides the rules. Cannot enter transactions.
Partner Reads everything and changes nothing. One login each, for all five.
The one idea worth understanding

The system bills days on a bus, not headcount. Every student has a start date, and when they leave, an end date. A student who joins on the 16th of a 30-day month is billed for 15 days, because that is how long they were carried. Everything about the way you enter students — always with dates, never editing history — follows from this, and it is why an invoice raised eight months ago can still be reproduced line for line.

§2

Setting up, once

Three screens. You do this when a new customer signs, and then rarely again.

Who Operations Accountant Administrator

2.1 Register the bus company

Bus companies → Add company

You enter
  • Name — as it should appear on their invoice
  • Code — your own short reference, e.g. BC-001
  • Address, contact person, phone — printed on the invoice
  • Payment terms — how many days they have to pay. Leave it at 15 unless their contract says otherwise
  • Status — Active
The system does

Nothing hits the accounts yet — a customer is not a transaction. From now on the company appears in the monthly invoice run, gets its own statement page, and every invoice raised for it inherits the payment terms you set here as its due date.

2.2 Register its buses

Buses → Add bus — once for each bus.

You enter
  • Which company it belongs to
  • Bus number and plate
  • Route name — this prints on the invoice, so use a name the customer recognises
  • Capacity, driver, device serial
The system does

Each bus becomes one line on that company's invoice. Give the routes names your customer would use — "Karrada, morning" rather than "Route 3" — because they are what the customer reads when they check the bill.

2.3 Put the students on the buses

Students → Enrol student

You enter
  • Name and student reference
  • School, if you track it
  • Company, then bus — the bus list narrows to that company's buses
  • Joined on — the date they first travelled. This is the field that decides the money.
The system does

Opens a dated record: this student, on this bus, from this date. Nothing else you do needs to know about billing — from this one date the system works out how many days of each month to charge for, now and every month afterwards, until you tell it the student left.

Loading students in bulk at the start

When a company first signs, you will be entering a hundred students at once. Set the joined on date to the day service actually starts, not the day you happen to be typing. If you type today's date for a service that began on the 1st, the first invoice will be short and you will have to correct it.

§3

The daily work

This is the entire day-to-day job. Three things happen to students; nothing else needs doing between one month-end and the next.

WhoOperations

3.1 A new student starts

Students → Enrol student — exactly as in §2.3. Give the date they started. The system will charge the customer from that day, part month and all.

3.2 A student leaves

Students → open the student → Record as left

You enter
  1. Their last day on the bus
  2. Why — left, transferred, or suspended
The system does

Closes the dated record on that day. The next invoice bills up to that date and no further. Their history stays exactly as it was, so last month's invoice still shows them.

Do not just change their status to "left"

Changing the status alone will not stop the billing. The system charges for the dated record, not the status, so the customer will keep being invoiced for that student until you give an end date. Always use Record as left.

3.3 A student moves to a different bus

Students → open the student → Edit → change the bus. That is all.

You enter

The new bus. Nothing else.

The system does

Closes yesterday's record on the old bus and opens a new one on the new bus from today. The invoice then shows the student on both buses, each for its part of the month, and the two parts add up to one month. The customer is not charged twice.

3.4 Someone who left comes back

Open them and use Re-enrol. Give the date they returned and the bus. A fresh record opens from that date; the gap while they were away is not billed.

§4

Invoice, payment, receipt

The cycle the first edition left undefined. This section specifies it end to end: how the invoice comes into existence, what is on it, how money coming in is recorded, what the bus company is handed as proof, and what happens when they pay part of it, pay it late, or never pay at all.

WhoAccountant Can see, cannot touch AdministratorPartner

4.1 Creating the month's invoices

Invoices → Generate month — once a month, usually on the last working day. Nobody types an invoice. The system builds them from the student register.

  1. Choose the month you are billing at the top of the screen.
  2. Read the preview. Every active bus company is listed with the students it will be charged for, the price, and the amount. Nothing has been written yet — this is a dry run.
  3. Check the companies the system says it will skip. Each one gives a reason in plain words: already invoiced, no price set for that month, no students, or the school year is paused for the summer. A skipped company is never a silent omission.
  4. Untick any company you do not want to bill in this run.
  5. Set the invoice date. It defaults to the last day of the month.
  6. Press Generate draft invoices.
You did

Picked a month, glanced down a list, pressed one button.

The system did

For every company: walked its buses, found every student whose dated record overlaps that month, counted the days each was carried, looked up the price in force for that company in that month, and produced a draft invoice with one line per bus. Draft invoices have not touched the accounts.

Running it twice is safe

A company already invoiced for a month is skipped rather than billed again, so you cannot create duplicates by re-running it. Drafts can be deleted freely — until you issue them, they do not exist as far as the accounts are concerned.

4.2 What is on the invoice

Each invoice is one page of A4, on ALLVA letterhead, and contains:

On the pageWhere it comes from
Invoice numberIssued automatically in sequence — INV-2026-00014. Never reused, never edited.
ALLVA's detailsName, address, phone, tax number, from Settings.
Bus company's detailsName, address, contact, tax number, from their record.
Issue date, due dateThe issue date you chose; the due date is that plus their payment terms.
Service periodThe month being billed, e.g. August 2026.
One line per busRoute name, number of students, months billed, price per student, line total.
Total dueThe sum of the lines, in whole dinars.
FootnoteThe price per student and how part months were worked out — so the customer can check the arithmetic themselves.

On screen, clicking any line opens the student-by-student breakdown behind it: every name, the days charged out of the days in the month, and a note such as "joined 16 Aug". This is the screen you open when a customer telephones to dispute a figure — it answers the question without anyone having to reconstruct anything.

4.3 Issuing and sending it

Review the drafts, then Issue all to release the whole month, or issue them one at a time from each invoice.

You did

Pressed Issue. Then Print, which opens the invoice on letterhead; print it, or save it as a PDF and email it.

The system did

Locked the invoice — issued invoices cannot be edited — and made the first accounting entry: the bus company now owes ALLVA this money, and ALLVA has earned this revenue. If you invoiced a month before it happens, the system holds the revenue back until that month arrives rather than counting it early. (Appendix B, B.1.)

4.4 When the bus company pays

Receipts → Record receipt

  1. Choose the bus company. The screen fills with their unpaid invoices, oldest first.
  2. Enter how much arrived, when, and how — cash, bank transfer, cheque, or other transfer.
  3. Enter the bank reference or cheque number, if there is one.
  4. Choose which cash or bank account the money went into.
  5. Press Apply oldest first and the system spreads the money across their open invoices for you — or type amounts against particular invoices if the customer has told you what they are paying for.
  6. Check the unallocated figure at the bottom, then Save.
You did

Entered an amount, a date and a reference, and pressed one button to match it.

The system did

Gave the receipt its number, recorded the money into the bank or cash account, reduced what the customer owes by the same amount, and marked each invoice it touched as part paid or paid. The customer's statement and the ageing report both update at once. (Appendix B, B.2.)

4.5 The receipt you give them

On the saved receipt, press Print receipt. It opens on the same letterhead as the invoice, so the two file together as a pair, and it contains:

  • Receipt number — RCP-2026-00021, issued in sequence like invoices
  • Received from and received by — both parties' details
  • The amount, in figures and in words — "480,000 IQD" and "Four hundred and eighty thousand Iraqi Dinars only", so a figure altered afterwards no longer agrees with itself
  • Date, method and reference — cash, transfer, cheque number
  • Which invoices it settled, how much went to each, and what is still due on each
  • The balance on their account after this payment, and a sentence saying in words whether they are now clear or still owe something
  • Signature lines for both sides, for the customer who wants one

Print it and hand it over, or save it as a PDF and email it. It can be reprinted at any time from the receipt's page — there is no single physical copy to lose.

Cash collected in person

For cash, record the receipt and print it before the money leaves the desk. The printed receipt is the customer's proof, and because it names the invoices it settles, there is no later argument about which month was paid.

4.6 When they pay only part of it

Enter what actually arrived. Apply it to the invoice. Nothing further is required.

You did

Recorded 300,000 against an invoice for 480,000.

The system did

Marked the invoice part paid, left 180,000 outstanding on it, and kept it in the unpaid list and in the ageing report at its original due date. The receipt they get says 300,000 received and 180,000 still due.

When the rest arrives, record a second receipt the same way. The invoice becomes paid and drops off the outstanding list. Both receipts stay on the record and both can be reprinted.

4.7 When they pay several months at once

Record the full amount received and apply only the part for which invoices exist. The rest stays on the receipt as unallocated — it is their money held on account, and the receipt says so.

As later months are invoiced, open that receipt again and apply the balance from the panel on its page. Unallocated cash is flagged on the receipts list so it cannot quietly sit there forgotten.

4.8 When they pay late

Nothing is required of you; the system is already tracking it. From the morning after the due date:

  • The invoice appears under Overdue on the invoice list
  • The dashboard names the number of overdue invoices and the total past due
  • The receivables ageing report puts the balance into a bucket — current, 1–30 days, 31–60, 61–90, or over 90 — so you can see at a glance who has been left too long
  • Anything over 90 days turns red on the dashboard

Chasing is done from the company's page, which shows their whole account — invoices raised, receipts applied, and the running balance — in one list you can read down the telephone.

Charging a late fee is not automatic

The chart of accounts has a place for late payment fees, but the system does not add them by itself, because whether ALLVA charges them is a commercial decision that is not settled. If you do charge one, it is entered as a separate line. If ALLVA decides late fees should be charged as a rule, say so and it can be automated — see §10.

4.9 When the money never comes

There is a difference between an invoice that was wrong and a debt that will not be paid, and the system keeps them apart:

SituationWhat you doWhat it means
The invoice should never have been raised Void it, with a reason The sale is cancelled. Revenue and the debt both come back off.
The invoice was right, the customer will not pay Write off balance, with a reason The sale stays — it happened. The debt becomes a bad debt, which is a cost of the business.

Both need a reason, both are permanent, and both stay visible. Writing off is deliberately a separate act from voiding, because a company that writes off a lot of debt is telling you something about its customers, and that should show in the accounts rather than disappear.

4.10 When you get something wrong

What happenedWhat to do
The invoice is still a draftDelete it, fix the student records, generate again.
It is issued but nothing is paidVoid it with a reason, then raise a corrected one.
It is issued and part paidVoid the receipts first, then the invoice. The system will not let you do it the other way round.
A receipt was entered for the wrong amountVoid the receipt and enter it again. The invoices it had settled reopen automatically.
A cheque bouncedVoid the receipt, giving that as the reason. The debt comes back.
Nothing issued is ever deleted

Once an invoice or receipt has been issued it stays on the record for good. Corrections are made by reversing — the system writes an equal and opposite entry and keeps both, with your name and reason on it. This is ordinary accounting practice, and it is the mechanism behind the promise in §1: an entry that can vanish is an entry that can be lost.

4.11 Seeing who owes what

QuestionWhere to look

§5

Recording what you spend

Money going out, entered the same way regardless of what it is for.

WhoAccountant

5.1 Any cost at all

Expenses → Record expense

You enter
  • What it was for — picked from a plain list: office rent, fuel, SIM cards, and so on
  • Which department paid for it
  • Is it for eTrackify, or for the company generally?
  • How much, and paid now or on credit
The system does

Files it to the right account, takes it out of the bank if it was paid, or records it as owed to a supplier if it was not. The eTrackify-or-general question is the one that lets the reports say whether the project itself makes money, separately from the cost of running the company.

5.2 The costs that run themselves

CostHow it gets in
Salaries Payroll → Prepare run pulls in every employee at their standard pay. Adjust any line for overtime or a bonus, approve, and post. Each employee can be given a payslip.
Equipment wearing out Anything bought that lasts — a laptop, a vehicle, tracking devices — is recorded as an asset and its cost spread over the months it is used, one button a month.
Cyber Gate's share Calculated by the system at month end. See §6.
§6

Closing the month

Once everything for the month is in, this is the whole of it. Do it in order — each step uses the figures the step above it produced.

WhoAccountant
  1. Check the student register is up to date. Everyone who joined or left this month must be recorded with the right dates. The invoices are built from this, so it comes first.
  2. Generate the month and issue the invoices (§4.1–4.3).
  3. Record every receipt that came in (§4.4).
  4. Record every cost, including supplier bills you have not paid yet. A cost left out makes the profit look bigger than it is, and the partners are about to be paid out of that profit.
  5. Run payroll and run depreciation — one button each.
  6. Post Cyber Gate's share. Revenue share → Prepare this period. The screen shows the revenue, the percentage, the share, and what ALLVA keeps. Press post. This must come after steps 2–5, because it is calculated from them.
  7. Check the books. Three reports, three green answers: trial balance balanced, balance sheet balanced, cash flow with nothing unexplained. If any of them complains, sort it out before going on.
  8. Close the period. Periods → Close. The month is now fixed and cannot drift.
If it refuses to close

There is an unposted draft entry sitting in the month, and the system tells you how many. An entry left in limbo in a closed month is precisely the lost item this system exists to prevent. Post them or delete them, then close.

6.1 Paying the partners

Not monthly. When the partners decide to take profit out — usually each quarter or once a year — the accountant runs Distributions → New distribution for the period they agreed, optionally holds some back in the company, and posts it. Each partner is then paid from their own row as the money goes out.

On 8 September the accountant records the transfer, applies it to the invoice, and prints RCP-2026-00021 — it states 300,000 received, "Three hundred thousand Iraqi Dinars only", the invoice it settled, and 180,000 still outstanding. Al-Noor's invoice sits in the unpaid list at its original due date of 15 September.

The cash arriving in September does not change August's profit

The two receipts in September turn a debt into cash — they move money from "owed to us" into the bank. This is why the profit report and the bank balance rarely agree, and why both exist.

Where the odd dinar goes

80,000 divides by five exactly. When it does not — and most months it will not — the system splits it so the five shares add back to the declared total to the dinar, giving the leftover unit to the largest fraction. Five shares that add up to one dinar less than what was declared would be exactly the kind of small unexplained gap this system exists to prevent.

What the operator actually did, all month

The whole of the human work
  1. Kept the student list current as children joined and left
  2. Pressed Generate month, glanced at the preview, pressed Issue all
  3. Emailed the invoice
  4. Recorded two receipts and printed two receipts
  5. Entered the month's bills
  6. Pressed payroll, depreciation, revenue share, close
What the system did without being asked
  1. Counted 120 students across 3 buses and priced them
  2. Made every ledger entry listed in Appendix B, each one in balance
  3. Worked out Cyber Gate's 240,000 and set it aside as owed
  4. Tracked 480,000 from owed, to part paid, to settled
  5. Aged the overdue balance and flagged it on the dashboard
  6. Arrived at 80,000 net profit and divided it five ways to the dinar

At no point did anyone type an account code, decide which account a figure belonged in, or work out a percentage. That is the division this document is arguing for, and Appendix B lists every entry the right-hand column made, for the auditor who will want to check it.

What they cannot do

A partner cannot create, edit, post, issue, void, or delete anything at all. The screens that do those things are not merely hidden from them — the system refuses the request. They also cannot change the price list, add users, or alter settings.

Why partners can read the audit trail

Deliberately, and it is worth stating plainly to the partners themselves. Seeing every entry but not who made it, or what was quietly changed afterwards, is not visibility of the company's financial activity. The audit trail is what turns the promise in §1 into something a partner can verify rather than take on trust.

§10

Three decisions still open

Each changes the numbers. The system does both answers to each and ships with one chosen — but a default is an assumption, not a decision. These should be settled before the first real month is accounted for.

10.1 What Cyber Gate's 50% is 50% of

Half of revenue — currently set

Cyber Gate takes 240,000 of the 480,000 before any of ALLVA's costs. ALLVA's 400,000 of costs then come out of its own 240,000, leaving 80,000 for the partners.

Half of profit

Costs come off first. On the same month, 480,000 less 160,000 of ALLVA's own costs leaves 320,000, split 160,000 each — and the partners get 160,000, not 80,000.

The difference in the worked example is 80,000 IQD a month on one customer. This is the most consequential open item in the document and should be confirmed against the signed agreement with Cyber Gate, not assumed.

10.2 Part months

When a student joins or leaves partway through a month, the customer is charged:

OptionA student carried 15 days of 30 pays
By the day — currently set2,000 — exactly the days they travelled
A full month regardless4,000 — present for a day, pay for the month
Half or whole month2,000 — half, because they were there half the month or less

Whichever is chosen applies to every bus company alike, and is printed on the invoice.

10.3 The summer

Whether July and August are billed is set per school year. The system currently bills all twelve months. If service genuinely stops over the summer, the year should be set to pause, and those months are then skipped rather than billed at zero.

10.4 A fourth question this edition raises

Late fees

§4.8 tracks overdue invoices but does not charge for them. There is an account waiting for late payment fees, but nothing calculates one, because it is not settled whether ALLVA charges them, at what rate, or after how many days. If the answer is that late fees should be charged as a matter of course, that is a small addition and should be specified here rather than left to whoever is chasing the payment.

Appendix A

Chart of accounts

Back-end reference. No operator ever types one of these codes — the system chooses the account from what you entered in plain words. They are set down here for the accountant and the auditor.

Seventy-three accounts in nine classes. The class number tells you what kind of thing an account is, and the reports are built from the classes, not from individual accounts, so adding a sub-account inside a class never breaks a report.

B.9 · The rules the ledger enforces

These are refusals, not warnings — the entry does not go through. An operator following §2 to §6 will rarely meet one, because the system fills these fields in for them.

The ruleWhy
Debits must equal creditsThe definition of a valid entry.
The month must be openA closed month is settled; it does not move.
A cost account needs a department and a projectWithout them, project profitability cannot be worked out at all.
Nothing may be posted to a heading accountHeadings are for grouping in reports, not for holding money.
One side per lineA line that is both a debit and a credit cannot be read.
A posted entry cannot be deleted, only reversedThe promise in §1.
A reversal cannot itself be reversedPost a fresh entry instead; otherwise the trail becomes unreadable.
A month with unposted drafts cannot be closedAn unposted entry stranded in a closed month is exactly the lost item this system prevents.
A receipt cannot be applied for more than an invoice owesIt would create a balance that does not exist.
Rate changes, reversals, voids and reopenings need a reasonThey go into the audit trail, and a reason given a year later is worthless.

C.2 · Invoice statuses

StatusMeaning
DraftGenerated but not issued. Has not touched the accounts. Can be deleted.
IssuedPosted. The customer owes it. Cannot be edited, only voided.
Part paidSome money has been applied; a balance remains.
PaidSettled in full.
OverdueIssued or part paid, and past its due date. A view rather than a state.
VoidCancelled with a reason; the entry was reversed. Stays visible.
Written offThe sale stands; the debt was given up as uncollectable.

C.3 · Month statuses

StatusMeaning
OpenEntries can be posted into it.
ClosedRefuses new entries. An accountant or administrator can reopen it, with a reason that is recorded.
LockedPermanent. Never reopens. Used once a year has been audited.

C.4 · What the audit trail keeps

Every action, against the user, with the date and time. Nothing in it can be edited or deleted by anyone, including an administrator. Alongside every posting and reversal it records price changes with their reason, ownership changes with their reason and effective date, role changes and deactivations, each settings change individually, month closures and reopenings, and sign-ins.
